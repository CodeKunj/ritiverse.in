2. Problem We Solve

Growing businesses outgrow their first tools: Excel sheets, WhatsApp chats, email, a basic website. The symptoms are scattered information, repeated manual work, missed leads, disconnected systems, and simple changes that take too long. Off-the-shelf software forces them to adapt their process. Traditional agencies deliver an interface but leave the owner dependent on developers.

3. Our Solution and Positioning

We build the digital infrastructure behind the screen: the website or app the customer sees, plus the CRM, ERP, admin panel, and workflows the team controls.

Differentiators to protect and prove:

  1. Fit over features: we design around the client's workflow, not our template.
  2. Business-controlled platforms: admin panels so owners change content and data without calling a developer.
  3. Connected systems: website, CRM, and operations sharing data instead of living in silos.

4. Target Customers

We want to serve all business types, which is fine as a long-term vision. For the first 12 months, though, I recommend a beachhead: one or two segments where we do our first 3 to 5 projects and build reusable modules and case studies. Everyone else is still welcome, but we don't market to them yet.

5. Offerings and Packaging

Selling seven things at once dilutes the message. Suggested structure:

Flagship offers (lead with these):

  • Business System Starter: CRM + admin panel + one connected website or workflow, scoped as a fixed-price pilot.
  • Website + Lead Engine: website with enquiries flowing into a simple CRM.

Expansion offers (sell after the first project): ERP modules, mobile app, data/API integration, automation, ongoing support.

Recurring revenue (important for stability):

  • Maintenance and support retainer
  • Hosting and managed infrastructure
  • Monthly improvement hours ("Evolve" stage in our process)

7. Go-to-Market Plan

Goal for the first 90 days: 2 to 3 signed pilot projects and 1 publishable case study.

  1. Warm network first. List 50 people the team knows who run or work in businesses. Ask for introductions, not sales. Most early clients come from here.
  2. Free "Business Systems Audit." A 30-minute call where we map their tools and show 3 improvements. It builds trust and produces discovery work.
  3. Fix the brochure. Replace placeholder contact details, add a specific call to action ("Book a free 30-minute discovery call"), and add one sample scenario or mini case study.
  4. Simple online presence. A website (we should be our own best demo), a LinkedIn page, and a Google Business profile.
  5. Partnerships. Accountants, CA firms, business consultants, and hardware/IT vendors who meet growing businesses and can refer them.
  6. Local presence. Business associations, industry meetups, and chambers of commerce.
  7. Demo assets. One demo CRM and one demo admin panel that prospects can click through.

8. Delivery Model

We follow the seven-step journey from the brochure: Understand, Define, Design, Build, Test, Go Live, Evolve. To make it a differentiator, commit to:

  • A written scope and timeline before build starts
  • Weekly progress demos
  • A handover pack (documentation, admin training) so clients are not dependent on us to change content
  • A 30-day post-launch support window

Internal standards to set early: a reusable project template, a reusable component library (login, roles, dashboards, forms, reports), code review, and a standard tech stack [decide as a team]. Reusable modules are how we improve margins over time.

10. Financial Plan (Simple Model) [ASSUMPTION]

Startup and monthly costs to estimate: salaries or founder stipends, tools and software licenses, cloud/hosting, marketing, legal and registration, accounting, workspace, and equipment.

Illustrative break-even logic (replace with your numbers):

  • Monthly fixed costs: ₹X
  • Average project value: ₹Y
  • Gross margin on projects: about 50 to 60%
  • Projects needed per month to break even: X ÷ (Y × margin)

Example: with monthly costs of ₹3 lakh, average project value of ₹2 lakh, and 55% margin, you need about 3 projects per month, or a mix of projects plus retainers. This shows why recurring support revenue and reusable modules matter.

Cash rule: always keep at least 3 months of operating costs in reserve, and never start a project without the advance payment.
